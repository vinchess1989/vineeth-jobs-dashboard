GPU Physical Implementation Engineer - GPU / Physical Design / ASIC / PNR / Power & Performance




We are currently partnered with a leading global technology company developing advanced GPU and semiconductor technologies for next-generation computing, graphics, and connected devices.

As part of their continued investment in high-performance graphics architectures, they are looking to hire a GPU Physical Implementation Engineer to contribute to the implementation, optimisation, and delivery of advanced GPU designs.




Key responsibilities

Contribute to the physical implementation of advanced GPU designs from synthesis through place-and-route and final implementation
Develop, maintain, and optimise physical design methodologies, flows, and implementation processes
Work across teams to align physical implementation with GPU architecture, design, performance, power, and product requirements
Support the development of high-quality GPU systems that balance performance, power, area, and implementation complexity
Analyse and optimise GPU designs against timing, power, performance, and physical design targets
Execute and debug synthesis and PNR flows across complex SoC/GPU designs
Perform static timing analysis (STA) and investigate timing violations and implementation bottlenecks
Conduct power analysis and identify opportunities to improve GPU power efficiency




Key requirements

Master's degree or PhD in Computer Engineering, Computer Science, Electrical Engineering, or a related technical discipline
2+ years of relevant GPU experience within semiconductor, SoC, graphics, or related technology environments
Strong understanding of physical implementation methodologies for complex SoC designs
Experience with synthesis and PNR (place-and-route) tools, flows, and methodologies
Strong understanding of SoC design implementation and physical design methodologies
Knowledge of semiconductor process technology and its impact on physical implementation
Understanding of circuit design principles and semi-custom design methodologies
Experience with Static Timing Analysis (STA) and timing closure
